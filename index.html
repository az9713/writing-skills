2. The five families

The skills fall into five groups by the kind of text they produce. Skills in one group often call each other. Skills in different groups rarely do.

3. Three pipelines: skills that hand work to each other

Nonfiction book

author-interview→book-writing→kdp-publishing

book-writing runs the whole book. When the author has no topic, no notes, no writing sample, or no story for a section, it calls author-interview (at its §0, Phase B, §5.2, or an [AUTHOR EXPERIENCE NEEDED HERE] marker). When the manuscript passes its Definition of Done (§22), §23 hands off to kdp-publishing.

STEM textbook

stem-textbook→ each chapter →rigorous-explainer→ each draft →science-editor

stem-textbook plans the whole book and keeps its state. For the craft of each chapter it reads rigorous-explainer (template, check scripts, teaching rules). For review of each drafted chapter it invokes science-editor. Its own table names the split: plan and state here, chapter craft there, rigor review in the editor.

Article

/writing-fragments→ the pile →/writing-shapeor/writing-beats

The fragments skill is the explore step: it collects raw pieces into one markdown file and imposes no order. The shape and beats skills are two exploit steps: each reads that file as a fixed pile and builds a separate article from it. Pick one of the two, not both, for one article.

The style skills sit outside all three pipelines. no-ai-slop can edit the output of any pipeline. asd-ste100 changes Claude's own replies, not your manuscript.

4. The 14 skills, one card each

Line counts are for SKILL.md only. Tags: type /name means Claude cannot start the skill by itself; you must type its name.

book-writing

nonfiction 2,243 lines · no extra files

Job: co-author a nonfiction book with a human author. The AI plans and drafts; the author keeps control of the thesis, sources, structure, voice, examples, and final judgment.

How: §0 starts from nothing (plain words, a book/ folder, an intake interview). Then: market check and positioning (Phase 0), book specification (A), sources (B), author persona (C), concept system (D), architecture (E), chapters (F), detailed outline (G), section-by-section drafting (H), reviews (I, J), 13 editorial passes (K), and optional production and publishing (§23). Author approval gates stop the work between phases.

Rule it will not break: it never invents author experience, sources, quotes, or reviews.

Not for: fiction, or a graduate STEM textbook.

author-interview

nonfiction 103 lines

Job: interview a would-be author one plain question at a time and save their words, unedited, as book material in book/interviews/YYYY-MM-DD-<mode>.md.

Four modes: topic (find 2–3 topics the author can write honestly), knowledge (source notes in the author's words), story (facts for one personal story, then a draft the author approves), voice (a writing sample for an author who has none).

Limits: 6–8 questions per session. The AI's summary is kept apart from the author's answers.

kdp-publishing

nonfiction 84 lines

Job: walk a first-time author through Amazon KDP in 8 steps: account and tax, the AI-content question, ISBN, interior format, cover, book details, price, preview and publish.

Split of work: the author does every step that needs their account, identity, or money. The AI explains, drafts text, and checks.

Caveat: its KDP facts were checked on the KDP Help Center on 2026-09-17 (for example, the 70% eBook royalty band is $2.99–$12.99 from 2026-07-07). KDP rules change; check again before relying on them.

stem-textbook

STEM 153 lines · 6 references · 1 script

Job: co-author a graduate textbook in science, maths, or engineering, from book specification to final manuscript.

How: 8 phases with author gates A–G: specification; a graph of results plus a book-wide notation table; architecture; detailed outline; drafting by section; chapter review by science-editor; book-level passes; final integrity. State lives in BOOK-STATE.yaml so a new session can resume.

Reader it assumes: a graduate fluent in linear algebra, multivariable calculus, and probability, with close to zero exposure to the subfield.

Its Origin section points to local files in the author's working folder that are not in this repo.

rigorous-explainer

STEM 338 lines · 6 references · 16 scripts · HTML template

Job: write one self-contained technical lesson, usually an HTML chapter with MathJax.

How: start from a problem that matters, define every term and symbol before use, derive each claim under stated assumptions, interpret it with a worked example, and keep theory apart from empirical or implementation evidence. The Python scripts check links, maths rendering, proofs, prose, figures, and code. It can also publish to GitHub Pages.

science-editor

STEM 212 lines · 2 references · evals

Job: act as a senior Springer textbook editor. It judges whether a graduate reader new to the subfield could learn from the pages.

The five-part test for every claim: a precise statement; every term defined; a proof in the smallest case that shows the mechanism; the rescue or limit case; a tie to something concrete (code, a figure, a measured number, a page of a paper).

Output: a verdict, blocking defects located as file:line with the full replacement text, line-level rewrites, structural notes, and what already works. It first loads a domain brief for the manuscript.

no-ai-slop

style 101 lines · eval.md · by Peter Yang, MIT

Job: two modes. Edit (default): make the smallest edit that removes AI patterns and keeps the writer's voice, then list what changed. Detect: name each pattern found, quote the line, and give a short fix, with no rewrite and no guess about whether AI wrote it.

Catches: a banned-word list (delve, leverage, robust…), binary contrasts ("not X, it's Y"), throat-clearing openers, colon reveals, importance puffery, weasel attribution, fake-profound endings, em-dash clusters, and more.

asd-ste100

style 86 lines

Job: make every reply follow ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English for the rest of the session: at most 20 words in an instruction, 25 in a description; active voice; one word, one meaning; no idioms; numbered procedures; a warning before its step.

Rule it will not break: it copies code, paths, file.py:42 references, numbers, and error text exactly. It stops only on "stop STE" or "normal mode".

writing-fragments

article type /name 79 lines · mattpocock/skills, MIT

Job: the explore step. Interview you hard about a subject and append every usable piece (a sharp sentence, a claim, a story, a half-thought, a quote) to one markdown file, separated by ---. No outline, no headings.

Most valued piece: a leading word, one coined term that names the whole idea.

writing-shape

article type /name 79 lines · mattpocock/skills, MIT

Job: an exploit step. Read the pile, agree what the reader already knows, offer 2–3 openings with different theses, then grow the article paragraph by paragraph. It argues about the form of each block (prose, list, table, callout) and never edits the pile.

writing-beats

article type /name 67 lines · mattpocock/skills, MIT

Job: an exploit step, choose-your-own-adventure style. At each point it offers 2–3 candidate next beats (one move each: a scene, a point, a question, a twist), you pick one, and it writes only that beat.

Shared rule with writing-shape: a concept must be grounded (known before the start, or set up by an earlier beat) before a beat can use it.

writing-for-agents

tech docs 81 lines + SKILL-MECHANICS.md · mattpocock/skills, MIT

Job: a reference for writing any document an agent reads: a skill, CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md.

Key ideas: context pointers (a skill description is one), context load versus human cognitive load, what to keep in the file versus push to a linked file, clear completion criteria for each step, leading words, stating the wanted behaviour instead of a prohibition, and pruning stale lines.

technical-docs-writer

tech docs 158 lines · 3 references · evals

Job: write the full documentation set for a software project in 5 phases: audit, plan the structure, write, validate, keep the docs true. Its conventions are drawn from Google, Stripe, AWS, and Vercel docs.

Output: a docs/ tree (overview, quickstart, onboarding, concepts, guides, reference, architecture, troubleshooting, and a user guide when non-developers use the product). Every file complete: no stubs, no "TBD".

dev-journey

tech docs 180 lines

Job: write a full record of one build session, from the first prompt to the finished product: decisions, tools, agents, every error text verbatim, near-misses, costs and limits hit, and unfinished work.

Timing: write it at the end of the session, from the live transcript, before the context is summarized. Claude may offer it once at the end of a long build.

5. Overlaps and how to choose

Each pair below does related work. The question under each pair decides which skill to use.

book-writing vs stem-textbook

Both plan and draft a whole book with approval gates, and both came from the same source workflow. They differ in what the book is built from. book-writing uses an author persona, stories, word budgets, and market positioning. stem-textbook uses a graph of results, a notation table, proofs, and page or lecture-hour budgets. stem-textbook lists "nonfiction habits in a STEM book" as a common mistake, because persona and anecdote push out derivation steps.
Ask: does the book have to prove or derive its claims? Yes → stem-textbook. No → book-writing.

stem-textbook vs rigorous-explainer

Both write rigorous chapters, and stem-textbook uses rigorous-explainer's template and scripts. rigorous-explainer does one lesson with no book-wide state. stem-textbook adds the whole-book layer: a shared notation table, results that depend on earlier chapters, BOOK-STATE.yaml, and gates.
Ask: is this one standalone piece, or chapter N of a book? One piece → rigorous-explainer.

science-editor vs no-ai-slop

Both edit a draft. science-editor checks substance: claims, proofs, definitions, numbers, citations. no-ai-slop changes only how the prose reads and adds no claims. science-editor's own description says to prefer it when claims, proofs, notation, or citations must be checked.
Ask: could a sentence be wrong, or only badly written? Wrong → science-editor. Badly written → no-ai-slop. For a technical chapter, run science-editor first, then no-ai-slop on the fixed text.

science-editor vs book-writing's editorial passes

book-writing runs its own 13 passes, including Pass 7 (fact-checking and citations). Those passes suit general nonfiction. They do not apply the five-part proof test.
Ask: is there mathematics to check? Yes → science-editor.

writing-shape vs writing-beats

Both read the same kind of pile, share the grounding rule, and write a separate article file. writing-shape commits to an opening and thesis, then fills the structure paragraph by paragraph. writing-beats keeps the route open and offers a fork at every step.
Ask: do you already know the argument? Yes → writing-shape. You want to find the path as you go → writing-beats.

author-interview vs writing-fragments

Both get material out of your head by questioning you and save it to a file. author-interview saves your words unedited, question by question, for a book, and never adds a fact. writing-fragments saves pieces from both sides of the conversation, in any shape, for an article.
Ask: book or article? Book → author-interview (book-writing calls it). Article → writing-fragments.

asd-ste100 vs no-ai-slop

Both change sentences, not content. asd-ste100 is a mode: it controls Claude's replies for the whole session. no-ai-slop is a one-time edit of a draft you give it. Their rules can conflict: STE removes idioms, metaphors, and rhythm; no-ai-slop keeps the writer's humour, digressions, and cadence.
Ask: are you changing Claude's replies or your own text? Replies → asd-ste100. Your text → no-ai-slop.

technical-docs-writer vs dev-journey vs writing-for-agents

All three write documents about software work, for different readers. technical-docs-writer writes lasting docs for people who use or build the code. dev-journey writes the story of one session. writing-for-agents covers documents an agent reads. The first two meet at one file: technical-docs-writer's history/development-journey.md is the place a dev-journey document belongs, and it links an existing one rather than recreating it.
Ask: who reads it? A developer or user → technical-docs-writer. A future you, auditing one session → dev-journey. An agent → writing-for-agents.

writing-for-agents vs skill-creator (outside this inventory)

writing-for-agents covers how to word a skill. The separate skill-creator skill (not in this repo) creates, tests, and benchmarks skills.
Ask: are you writing the words, or building and testing the skill? Words → writing-for-agents.

6. How each skill starts

Eleven skills are model-invoked: Claude reads their description and can start them when your request matches, and you can also type the name. Three are user-invoked: writing-fragments, writing-shape, and writing-beats set disable-model-invocation: true. Claude cannot start them and no other skill can call them. Type /writing-fragments, /writing-shape, or /writing-beats.

Three skills are persistent or proactive. asd-ste100 stays on until "stop STE" or "normal mode". dev-journey may offer itself once at the end of a long build. book-writing and stem-textbook keep state files so work resumes across sessions.

Install

Copy a skill folder from skills/ in this repo into ~/.claude/skills/. Keep the folder name. Copy the partner skills too when a skill calls them: book-writing needs author-interview and kdp-publishing; stem-textbook needs rigorous-explainer and science-editor.
